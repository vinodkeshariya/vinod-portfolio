"Do you use AI to build websites?" is one of the first questions clients ask me now. The short answer is yes — every day. The more useful answer is *how*, because the way a developer uses AI decides whether you get a faster project or a fragile one.

Since June 2023 I've been building, migrating and maintaining client websites at Krushna53 with Next.js, React, Sanity CMS and WordPress. Over that time AI tools moved from "interesting" to a normal part of my workflow. Here's exactly where they help, and where I deliberately keep the work in my own hands.

## The tools I actually use

- **Claude** — thinking through requirements, planning features and explaining unfamiliar code.
- **Claude Code** — working directly inside a codebase: building features, creating components, debugging.
- **ChatGPT** — quick questions and second opinions.
- **MCP** — connecting AI tools to the services and data a project already uses.
- **Cursor** — AI help while editing code.

None of these replace the project. They speed up specific steps inside it.

## Where AI speeds things up

### 1. Understanding requirements and planning

Before writing code, I use AI to break a brief or a Figma design into pages, components and content types, and to spot questions I need to ask the client early. A clearer plan at the start means fewer surprises later.

### 2. Building components and repetitive code

A lot of web development is repetitive: similar React components, form handling, layout variations, TypeScript types. AI is very good at producing a solid first version of this kind of code, so I can spend my time on the parts that are specific to your project.

### 3. Working inside existing codebases

Many projects aren't new — they're an existing site that needs a feature, a fix or a cleanup. Claude Code helps me understand how an unfamiliar codebase is organised, find where a change belongs and trace bugs faster.

### 4. CMS and migration work

Setting up Sanity CMS schemas, modelling content, connecting APIs and moving content from WordPress all involve a lot of careful but repetitive steps. AI helps me move through them faster.

## Where I don't rely on AI

This is the part that matters most for you as a client.

- **Reviewing the code.** Every piece of AI-generated code is read and understood before it goes into your project. If I can't explain why it works, it doesn't ship.
- **Debugging and verifying.** AI can suggest a fix; I confirm the real cause and check that the fix doesn't break something else.
- **Testing the result.** I test the finished site across browsers and devices myself — layouts, forms, content editing and performance.
- **Choosing the right tool.** Whether your project needs Next.js, Sanity or WordPress is a decision based on your requirements, your team and your budget — not on what's trendy or what an AI suggests by default.

AI-generated code can look correct and still miss an edge case, a design detail or the way your content is actually structured. That's exactly why review and testing stay in my hands.

> AI accelerates the process. Engineering judgment ensures the quality.

## What my process looks like

1. **Understand requirements** — your goals, design, current website and content.
2. **Plan** — technology, structure, timeline and estimate.
3. **AI-assisted development** — building faster with the tools above.
4. **Human code review** — every change checked by me.
5. **Testing** — responsive layouts, functionality and fixes.
6. **Deployment** — launch, with preview links shared along the way.

## What this means for your project

- **Less time spent on the repetitive parts**, so more attention goes to the parts that make your site work for your business.
- **Regular progress updates and preview links**, so you see the work as it develops.
- **Code that a person has read, understood and tested** — not something generated and shipped blind.
